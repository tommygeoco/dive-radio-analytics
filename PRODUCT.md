# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Dive Radio's hosts and operators, primarily Tommy and Ridd. They open the dashboard between shows or after a data refresh to decide whether the show is healthy, whether the audience is growing, what helped the latest episode, and where promotion or format changes deserve attention.

## Product Purpose

Turn reliable episode, audience, and distribution stores into a few decisions that can be understood in seconds. The first view should answer what is happening now; deeper views should make every answer traceable without making the default page feel like an audit report.

## Brand Personality

Calm, candid, decisive. The product should feel like a trusted producer who has already done the analysis: direct about what is known, equally direct about what is missing, and sparing with the reader's attention.

## Anti-references

- KPI walls, control-room dashboards, and grids of equally weighted cards.
- Dense research reports with labels on every line and explanations competing with the finding.
- Tiny uppercase headings, badge clutter, symbol legends, hover-only meaning, or UI that requires the PRD to decode.
- Decorative dashboard styling that makes weak or missing data look more certain than it is.
- Text-heavy layouts that narrate the same fact in several places.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with the decision, then reveal the evidence.
2. Use visual comparison for patterns and prose only for the conclusion.
3. Give one surface one job; remove repeated labels and repeated numbers.
4. Make uncertainty visible without making it loud: missing is never zero, and thin evidence stays out of strong claims.
5. Keep source detail, methods, and exact definitions available on demand rather than permanently in the reading path.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast and full keyboard access. Important state must never depend on color, hover, or a symbol alone. Body copy and controls must remain comfortably readable, focus states must be visible, reduced-motion preferences must be honored, and responsive layouts must preserve the same decision order without horizontal page overflow.
